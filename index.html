Record blood pressure

For home BP readings, sit quietly for 5 minutes, keep feet flat and back supported, and position the cuffed arm at heart level. Avoid caffeine, smoking and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand. Follow your clinician's measurement instructions.

Record blood glucose

Fasting, pre-meal, post-meal, random glucose and HbA1c are different measurements. HbA1c shows your average sugar over roughly the last 3 months and comes from a laboratory test — you can also record it with the full report under Lab reports. A home meter reading cannot by itself diagnose diabetes.

Reference guide

BP: General adult categories: normal below 120/80; elevated systolic 120–129 with diastolic below 80; high BP stage 1 range 130–139 or 80–89; stage 2 range at least 140 or at least 90. Above 180 systolic and/or above 120 diastolic is very high: repeat after at least one minute and contact a clinician urgently if it remains high. With concerning symptoms, seek emergency care.

Glucose: Below 70 mg/dL is low; below 54 is clinically significant low glucose. For many non-pregnant adults with diabetes, common goals are pre-meal 80–130 and peak post-meal below 180 mg/dL, but targets are individualised. Fasting laboratory plasma glucose 126 mg/dL or above is in the diabetes diagnostic range and needs clinical confirmation.

HbA1c: average blood sugar over about 3 months. Below 5.7% is the usual reference; 5.7–6.4% is the prediabetes range; 6.5% or above is the diabetes range. Many adults with diabetes aim for below 7%, but your doctor sets your target.

Lab ranges: the pre-filled thyroid, liver, kidney and lipid ranges are typical adult values. Laboratories differ — always use the range printed on your own report.
